# My Song Writer

Lyric-first songwriting app. Start with words, a tune, or an accompaniment idea and build it out into a full song with melody, chords, bass, accompaniment, backing vocals and ensemble parts.

Single-file, zero dependencies, works offline: open `index.html` in any modern browser, or serve the folder for GitHub Pages. The notation is engraved in the standard music glyphs (SMuFL) from a subset of Steinberg's **Bravura** font, embedded in the page as a data URI, so clefs, key and time signatures, noteheads, flags, rests, accidentals, articulations and dynamics look the same on every device whatever fonts it has, and the note, rest, clef and accidental symbols in the tools, labels and messages (♩ ♪ 𝄽 𝄞 ♯ …) come from the same outlines, set on a text baseline; the subset is renamed *MSW Music* as the SIL Open Font License asks of a modified version, and the licence is in `BRAVURA-LICENSE.txt`.

## Using it

**Widescreen.** On a wide screen (a landscape tablet, a desktop, a phone turned sideways), with the View set to Rolled up (the default), the staves become a deck. The Rhythm strip on every layout is a taller silver band with black entries, flush between the stave and the Lyrics strip. With the View set to Stacked staves the wide screen shows two staves instead, on the deck's frame with its tool bar across the foot: the melody, with 4 bars (or 8, set in Settings) laid across the width and nothing to scroll, its Dynamics, Rhythm and Lyrics strips flush beneath it, and below them the accompaniment — or the bass, swapped in with the ⇄ button beside it or in Settings — spanning the same bars at about a third of the screen's height, flush on the tool bar, the melody taking everything above the strips; no title box and no part selectors, the bar bracket centred at the very top. The part selectors on the deck are check boxes that act at once: unchecked, a part is switched off and leaves the view; checked, it is on and in view; the last one checked stays, greyed, until another is checked. The deck itself: one part is in view at a time, the stave reaching almost to the screen's edges, a slim column beside it holding two small silver buttons at its top corner, ▲ and ▼, for the parts above and below it in the deck (their names in the buttons' tips) — tap one and that part slides into view; the tempo (− ♩= +) sits up in the head row between ⟳ and the bracket's ←. The stave takes the whole width, wearing its part's colour — bright green for Melody, rich yellow for Accompaniment, red for Bass — with the part's name in black on its top edge. Beneath it the Rhythm and Lyrics strips sit flush, one on the other, and their SHOW / HIDE buttons — DYN, RHYTHM, LYRICS — sit as a fixed set at the far left of the tool bar, never flipped or scrolled, each folding its strip away or bringing it back. The stave always fills the height, showing as many rows of pitches as fit at its usual scale — reaching lower when the range would run past the top — and sits flush on the strips, which sit flush on the tool bar; hidden, the stave meets the tool bar itself. The part row above, centred over the stave, has a check box per part (Melody, Accomp., Bass), each with a small gear centred past its name that opens that part's own settings, with the **ADD to VIEW** button in the column beside the stave: check the parts you want and press it, and those make up the deck; HIDE PARTS / SHOW PARTS, beside the save button near the gear, folds the whole row away and the stave takes the room, reaching up to just under the bar row. There are no beat labels on the deck. Beside the stave, in a column as tall as it, sit ▲ ▼ to the parts above and below (▲ flush with the stave's top), the part's gear close under them, then BACK, UNDO, REDO, COVER, SELECT, DELETE, REPEAT and COPY in the tool bar's own silvery finish (the Lyrics **EDIT** button sits on the left, in the strip's tag column); the skinny, short ↑ ↓ pitch-range arrows sit snug against the pitch labels at the far edge of the stave's own gutter, centred on its height (hold one for an octave) — a Settings switch locks how far they (and the swipe that does the same) may scroll, to each sound's own range, top and bottom alike, instead of the whole keyboard. The lyrics keyboard opens a fifth smaller, docked flush on the tool bar and centred, carrying only its letters; while it is open the bottom tool bar becomes the chip tool panel — one fixed row, nothing to flip, scroll or detach — left to right Multi-chip, Single chip, SPLIT, UNDO, REDO, NUDGE LEFT, NUDGE RIGHT, SHORTEN, WIDEN, REST CHIP, DELETE CHIP(S), SELECT CHIP(S), SET CHIP, NEW CHIP → NEXT BEAT, NEW CHIP – CURRENT BEAT and NEW CHIP ANY SPACE; closed, the tool bar's three thirds return. The keyboard itself stays on its line but can be pulled apart: square grippy handles at its top corners drag each half outward — parted between t and y, g and h, v and b, and the space bar halved — as far as the screen's edge, and a half stays wherever it is dropped. Each half's rows are five equal slots, so every letter key is one size on both halves and the rows line up square: on the left, z x c v sit under s d f g with the slot under a left blank, and the bottom row is ⇧ (letter-sized) beside ?!’, then àéñ and the space bar under f and g; on the right, h j k l and b n m sit under y u i o and h j k, and the bottom row holds a space bar the same width as the left half's and BACKSPACE, two keys wide at the right end — both labelled (moveable), and both slide: a two-second hold on the key (or, with a mouse, a double click) unseats it — it lifts — then drag it (the space bar left or right, BACKSPACE up its track into the blank corner above, its left half under l, or anywhere along the way), let go, and one plain tap on it sets it in place; a plain tap on a seated key types or deletes as ever. The two never overlap — the space bar only reaches the bottom right corner once BACKSPACE is fully up, and as BACKSPACE comes down the space bar gives way. A small ? tab at the right half's bottom right corner pulls out a ribbon that says so. Under the Flat layout every point of a half lands on a key: the pads reach across the row gaps and out to the half's edges. It covers whatever lies in its path. While it is open the Rhythm and Lyrics strips ride flush on the tops of its grips, chips and all, the stave taking their rows and reaching up over the part selectors, the bar bracket and the save button to the top of the screen; a ✕ CLOSE key sits in the column beside the stave, above the strip buttons. Each half of the keyboard is a white box of its own on a see-through frame, so pulling the halves apart shows the stave between them; closed, everything returns to its place. On the deck the Lyrics strip's font is larger (up to 1.3×, as the beats are wider than on a phone), its words, chips and entry box growing with it, the strip itself keeping its height and its rules. Chips keep their place in the bar whichever way the screen faces: a chip's beat time never moves when the phone turns or the window resizes — only the automatic squeezes are solved again for the new beat width (relaxing on a wider screen, squeezing to fit the beat on a narrower one, anything that still cannot fit drawn pushed along), so turning back restores the layout exactly. The bar bracket, narrower, rides at the very top of the screen between the menu and the gear, centred over the stave with the parts button beside it; there is no title box (the title is set in Song setup), the save button sits at the top right beside the gear, and the stave reaches up into the room this leaves. The tools run as a single bar, one button high, across the foot of the screen, in thirds, each with its own ⟳ flip button at its outer corner: the left third flips between its tools — MOVE under its BULK cap, then PITCH (▲ ▼) and ITEM (◀ ▶) — and the staff-lines switch; UNDO and REDO, SELECT, DELETE, REPEAT and COPY live in the column beside the stave. The left and middle thirds are only as wide as their tools, the play buttons packed to the left of theirs, so the notes palette follows close behind them, four keys in view and the rest a scroll away; WIDER and TIGHTER sit under a STAVE cap at the bar's right end, before its flip button, acting on the stave in view. SELECT picks a run of beats for MOVE or DELETE: MOVE's first tap claims it and arms — finding empty space the same size in view, if there is any, blinking blue with a green preview of where it would land; tapping a different spot with room enough (anywhere in view, including after paging to another bar) redirects that preview there instead — MOVE again confirms wherever is currently shown; DELETE's first tap arms it, blinking red — DELETE again confirms. REPEAT and COPY need no SELECT: tapping either starts its own picking. REPEAT copies the tapped beat(s) — a run, or a pattern with gaps — into the space right after them the moment REPEAT is tapped (tapping an empty beat first, instead, sends it there — the same beats of whichever bar that tap landed in, for a pattern with gaps), and tapping REPEAT again keeps copying into whatever space follows that, or wherever was tapped. COPY gathers what to copy two ways at once — tap beats or bars and press SET TO COPY to bank them, or turn on CROSSHAIR and drag a box over the stave itself, release setting it straight into the bank (a chord split by the box, or a note cut through by its edge, is left out — the rest of the box still banks, with a word if something was skipped) — up to five banked, each a complete note or chord, never part of one; then tap any empty beat with room to paste the whole banked set exactly as it read at the source. A CANCEL key (and, for COPY, CROSSHAIR and SET TO COPY) appears beside MOVE on the tool bar while one of these is in charge, pushing the rest of the row along rather than covering it — and a single switch, also set in Settings, for the solid orange box drawn around each melody note at its own duration (a beat of four 16th notes reads as four such boxes side by side); the beat lines inside the bars can be hidden in Settings — the bar lines stay and the spacing does not change; the middle third flips and scrolls between the playback controls (play all, ▶ BAR — the part in view, this bar — beside it, play range, stop, tempo) and, on its flip, ▶ SELECT and CHORD for the part in view, CHORD blinking slowly while chord mode is on; and the right third — which scrolls sideways when a palette is wider than it, the keys slipping out at its edge and back as it scrolls — flips, with the ⟳ button pinned at its corner, between four palettes: notes (the quick palette, here a fixed part of the bar with nothing to lock or float), rests, dynamics (crescendo, decrescendo and diminuendo from a note; mf sf f ff fff ffff, mp p pp ppp pppp on a note, carrying on from there in playback — a dynamic goes on the selected note, or, with none selected, the key arms it and the next taps put it on notes, or on the first note of a tapped beat, until the key is tapped again; the SHOW DYN button at the foot of the gutter beside the stave opens a Dynamics strip, silver like the Rhythm strip, flush under the stave above the Rhythm strip — shown or hidden on its own, whether or not the other strips are — showing the part's dynamics along the beats) and marks (accent, marcato, staccato, staccatissimo, tenuto — drawn above or below the head by the stem's direction). The whole screen fits without scrolling.

The screen is one bar at a time: one head row — ☰ menu, ⟳ (a fresh load of the app), the bar bracket, 💾 and ⚙ — the part selectors right under it, and the play buttons with the tempo in a pallet at the foot of the page; no title box (the title is set in Song setup). The bar bracket with ◀ ▶ and a dot per bar (it names the bar, and the beat in hand — a tapped beat, a selected note or chip, the beat the lyrics box is typing into, or a picked run's first beat), the beat row (tap a beat for its chord), the **Melody** window, the **Rhythm** strip, the **Lyrics** strip, a block of ↑ ↓ ← → arrows with UNDO / REDO and MOVE / DELETE beneath (DELETE blinks red when a note, a chip, picked chips or a picked run of beats are selected, and then deletes them; with nothing selected it starts a delete run: tap the first and last beats, then DELETE again), the note palette, then the **Accompaniment** and **Bass** windows. Beside the arrows, in the left half, sits a small **quick palette**: the 32nd, sixteenth and eighth notes beside the quarter along the top (the quarter at the top right), beneath them a quarter rest and the double whole, whole and half notes, and a third row with SLUR, TIE, the ghost note and the dot. A tap picks that length just as in the tools. Its 🔒 unlocks it: then hold a button and drop it on another and the two trade places (or the displaced one takes the nearest empty cell). ⧉ floats it so you can drag it by its grip anywhere on the screen; ⤶ puts it back. The 💾 beside the tempo opens the song library to save. Chords… lives in ☰ → Song setup. Each stave has a 🗑 at the bottom of its button column: it deletes the selected note (or word, on the melody) or clears the active beat. The arrows move the selected note (pitch up and down, earlier and later), nudge a selected word, shift a picked run, or step the active beat across bars; with nothing selected ↑ ↓ move the stave's register.

1. **☰ → New song…** — opens Setup for name, genre, key, time signature and tempo (a genre loads structure, chords and tempo); ☰ → Song setup changes them later. Setup also adds a **pickup** (a ½-, 1- or 2-beat bar before beat 1), or a **Custom** pickup: a full bar of the time signature to build any rhythm on the stave, then **Trim the unused end** (in Setup or the bar's Rhythm menu) cuts whatever follows the last note, word or chord change, to the ¼ beat; an empty pickup is removed, and **Open to a full bar** gives the room back to add more.
2. **Rhythm & notes** — one bar is awake at a time. Tap anywhere in a bar on any stave to wake it (tinted across all three, the beat you tapped a shade deeper); other bars are locked and a tap there just wakes that bar. Inside the awake bar: pick a length, tap once to show the orange box at the next free spot of the marked beat, then tap the pitch you want anywhere along the bar — the tap need not fall inside the marked beat, it gives the pitch and the box gives the time — and the note lands on that beat; when the beat is full the next beat of the bar with room takes it. Tap a note or rest to select it for the palette tools (and aim at its beat). Settings → Bar select highlight sets the awake bar's tint: the default box colour, light blue, gray, boxed only (a medium black frame, no tint) or none. Note lengths, the slur, the tie, the dot and the ghost note (an X head: it goes in softly, barely heard — tap it to arm the next note, or to toggle a selected note) are all on the quick palette beside the arrows. The top row's left corner holds the whole, half, eighth, 16th and 32nd rests (the quarter rest is on the quick palette): with a beat active a tap puts that rest at the next free spot (a whole rest is the bar; longer rests run on into the following bars as far as they are clear), on a note or rest you tapped yourself it becomes a rest of that length, and otherwise it arms that rest for your next tap on a stave or the Rhythm strip. Staccato and tenuto sit at the top right of the tools and the editing tools on the row below; MOVE and DELETE sit under UNDO and REDO beside the quick palette. A note longer than the room left in its beat is dealt out beat by beat, each piece the largest values that fill it, all tied: a half note on a beat is two tied quarters, a quarter on the "and" two tied eighths; it runs on into the following beats and bars while they are empty (in chord mode, whatever is already there). With a mark (staccato, tenuto, accent…) armed, a tap anywhere along a note marks it, however full the beat. Ties and slurs may still reach into locked beats. The Rhythm strip adds boxes without a pitch; a rest button arms a rest for the next box. ⌒ ties a note to the next (or into the next bar). Tap a box or the Rhythm strip to select a note and change its length, dot it, move or delete it.
3. **Melody** — tap a pitch inside a box to give that note its pitch, or tap an empty spot to add a note at the palette length. Notes align with the rhythm on their left edge. ▲▼ beside a window move its register (hold for an octave); ⚙ sets instrument, sound and extra voices, and the stave's paper shade: default, aged (tanned like old paper), light eggshell, or plain white.
4. **Words** — tap the Lyrics tag (or the strip): the keyboard opens with the entry box at the left edge of the strip. Type and press **NEW CHIP – CURRENT BEAT**: what you typed becomes a chip exactly as wide as the word (half a space of margin either side; the entry box itself ends just past the last letter typed, with a few pixels of slack so every letter stays in view), flush against the chip before it (edge to edge, running across beat lines if the word is long), and the box waits just after it, the chip just set staying selected (the panel's tools act on it) until you type again — a new chip never goes in before the right edge of the right-most chip. The beat it goes in is the one the last chip starts in (the beat you are working in, even when that chip runs a little past its line), and that beat's chips squeeze, down to where they still read, to make the room; when even that cannot fit the words, it asks: NEW CHIP ANY SPACE, modify the previous entry (your words wait and come back to the box once that chip is set), next available space (the chip goes flush after the last one), proceed (the words go to the beat line beyond), or cancel; **NEW CHIP → NEXT BEAT** makes the chip and moves the box to the next beat line — the line after the beat the last chip starts in, that beat's chips squeezing (down to where they still read) so nothing runs past it; when they cannot clear the line it asks: NEW CHIP ANY SPACE, modify the previous entry, next available space (flush after the last chip), proceed to the line beyond, or cancel, which puts everything back as it was before the tap. When this beat is full it first asks, Proceed or Cancel, since the words will be cleared from this beat and set at the start of the next. Below the letters sits the tool panel, four columns of rounded silver-blue keys (HIDE PANEL / SHOW PANEL in the header folds it away and back): the two modes, DELETE CHIP(S), SELECT CHIP(S), NUDGE LEFT / NUDGE RIGHT, SPLIT, SHORTEN, WIDEN, UNDO ↶ / REDO ↷, REST CHIP, the three NEW CHIP keys and SET CHIP. SELECT CHIP(S) turns green, takes the chip in progress into the selection first (the chip under the cursor is set as it stands, words still in the box become their chip, a ↔ chip settles), and taps on the strip then pick more chips (tap again to unpick); With chips picked, the tools act on all of them together: DELETE CHIP(S) removes them — and only them: the one way to delete is SELECT CHIP(S), tap the chip(s), DELETE CHIP(S) (pressed with nothing picked it only says so; a chip merely tapped or set is never enough on its own), NUDGE LEFT / NUDGE RIGHT move them as one, SHORTEN and WIDEN size each of them; with nothing picked, NUDGE LEFT / NUDGE RIGHT, SHORTEN and WIDEN act on the chip in hand, but DELETE never does — the bulk DELETE on the tool bar follows the same rule. As you type into a chip, the set chips that start in the same beat squeeze alike to make room, each down to about three quarters of its width where it still reads, and take it back as the text shrinks; past that limit the text runs on into the next beat. WIDEN works the same way: the chip in hand grows out to the next beat line beyond its edge, the chips before that line squeezing to make way; SHORTEN takes it back to its word's own width, then down to the least it can be. A chip still in the entry box is set first, so the sizing shows on the chip itself with its text condensed or spread; while a chip is in the box it shows at its word's own width. Deleting a chip selects its neighbour on the left (or the right, if none). **NEW CHIP ANY SPACE ↔** on the keyboard puts a blank spacer chip in hand, white with a dashed edge and a ↔ on it, in the middle of the bar (or the nearest free space): drag it along the strip and it clicks onto the edges of other chips and onto beat lines, never over a chip; SET CHIP sets it where it is, and so does starting to type, the words going into it. Set, it fills the room up to the next beat line or chip and is an ordinary chip from then on. It cannot be set over another chip, and one still in hand when the keyboard closes is set where it is, or dropped if that is not free. While the keyboard is open the staves still swipe up and down to slide their range (a tap on them sets the chip in hand, or the keyboard blinks). Nothing sits above the keyboard's letters but its ✕, a small box peninsula on its top edge (on the deck the ✕ CLOSE key is in the column beside the stave instead); the HIDE PANEL / SHOW PANEL key sits at the right of the space row (the phone's own keyboard is chosen in Settings, under Lyrics keyboard). While the keyboard is open it stays open for taps on the Lyrics strip: a tap on a chip brings that chip into the box to edit (a word being typed becomes its chip first), and a tap on empty space puts the box in that beat, as far left as the chips allow (the beat line, or flush after the last chip) — the empty box wears a white border to confirm the tap, though it is no selected chip: DELETE, NUDGE and the rest leave it be — for the next chip — the chips stay exactly as they are until something is typed. A tap anywhere else is held off: the keyboard blinks twice around its edge and its small ✕ flashes — that ✕ is what closes it. **SET CHIP**, styled like a chip on the strip, sets whatever is in hand as it is — a spacer still being placed, or the words in the box, and always as one chip, however many words are in the box (SPLIT parts it later if you want them apart; only the NEW CHIP keys follow the Multi-chip / Single chip mode) — and a tap off the keyboard does the same. The two mode keys at the panel's top left choose what the text becomes — **Multi-chip** (one chip per word, side by side; the selection each time the keyboard opens) or **Single chip** (the entry box itself stretches across the bar, and everything typed becomes one chip that fills it, the box moving on to the next bar). Choosing Single chip on a bar that already has chips asks: delete them and start fresh, combine them into one bar-wide chip with each word kept where it was, or cancel. Words have no bearing on the notes; drag a chip anywhere along the strip (it clicks onto beat lines when close; chips never overlap — pressed together they squeeze to about three quarters of their width and stay readable, and a bar too full for even that shrinks them all alike, side by side), and put spaces inside a word to spread its syllables under their notes. With the entry box empty, ⌫ backs over what is behind it: the chip there is removed; free space steps back a quarter beat (a step that would cross a beat line stops on the line), and the start of the bar steps to the bar before. Space with the box empty goes the other way: over the chip ahead to land just after it, else a quarter beat on (stopping on a beat line it would cross, so the next chip begins flush with the line), and the end of the bar steps to the next. Close the keyboard and the first word you typed is already selected. One tap on a chip (a one-letter chip such as I has a wider, invisible hit pad, so a tap beside it still lands on it) opens the keyboard, panel and all, with that chip in the box, the box hugging its words (the chip never grows into the room beside it); a chip with no more space to take (nothing free after it and its beat's chips squeezed as far as they read) is selected instead, with a message saying so, the keyboard still opening for its tools; the **EDIT** button beside the strip opens the keyboard too, on the selected chip when it has room, else at the left-most free spot of the bar (the palette itself only names the selected chip); SHORTEN and WIDEN on the keyboard size it — narrowed text condenses, widened text spreads its letters.
5. **Chords** — tap a beat in the beat row or use Chords… for a per-beat grid. A chord holds until the next change.
6. **Band** — Accompaniment and Bass are empty staves to write on, like the melody: a new song or a genre load brings chords, structure and tempo, nothing prefabricated — there are no band patterns in the app any more, and no ensemble part. ⚙ on each window sets instrument, sound and volume. Hand-placed notes tie within a bar and across the bar line like the melody's.
Articulation: • staccato and ‒ tenuto sit in the tools palette, ◠ slur on the quick palette. With a note selected, a tap applies the mark; otherwise a tap arms it so entered notes carry it and tapping existing notes marks them, and a second tap returns to note entry. Slurs: tap the first note, then the last. Playback shortens staccato, holds tenuto and plays slurred notes legato.

Notes follow standard engraving: eighths and shorter are beamed per beat (per dotted quarter in 6/8), sixteenths get a second beam and each halving after that one more, stems lengthen to carry extra flags or beams, a double whole has its two bars, and stems point down above the middle of the window.

Windows share the screen: the melody gets the most room and Accompaniment and Bass split the rest. The small button under each window's ⚙ cycles it open → half → closed (one window always stays open) and the others take up the space. The view is never pinched: the rows' spacing is set from WIDER / TIGHTER and the bars in view from Settings (the two-finger pinch is put away behind `PINCH_ENABLED` in index.html), and the page itself does not zoom. On a wide screen the ⛶ button under a window's controls gives that window the whole screen; tap it again to go back.

7. **⚙ Settings** — view: **rolled up** (the default — Melody, Accompaniment and Bass as three labelled bars across the top with + / − to open and close each stave; the melody starts open; on a wide screen this is the deck, one part at a time) or **stacked staves** (all three at once, on any screen) or stacked staves; theme (default, dark, custom colours), window shows 1, 2, 3 or 4 bars (more bars widen the staves; everything scrolls together), window sizing (small, medium, large: the row height and how much of the screen the staves may take), chords displayed (names / Roman / hidden), note appearance (traditional / pills / circles), note size (micro, small, medium, large: heads, stems and flags scale together and never outgrow the space they sit in — micro also lays every bar the window shows across it side by side, with nothing to scroll, so with Window shows at 2, 3 or 4 bars several can be seen and edited at once, upright or widescreen), half steps on lines, lock the register scroll to each sound's own range, keep the screen awake while the app is open (on by default; the phone stops dimming and sleeping once you have touched the app, where the browser allows it), lyrics font, the app keyboard's layout (Flat: square corners, the letter keys flush together while the space bars, BACKSPACE, ⇧, ?!’ and àéñ keep their spacing; Standard, the default: rounded, spaced keys) and its colour theme (the current look; light blue with black keys and white letters; black with white keys and black letters; dark grey with light blue keys and white letters), metronome and count-in.
8. **☰ Menu / Save…** — share a link, per-musician parts and performance mode; export/import files; save, open, copy and start songs.

The dots under the bar bracket show each bar of the section: green when every voice is full, white when empty, yellow in between.

## Five-line focus

Each stave's ⚙ → **Five-line focus** covers the stave white outside the five middle lines — nine rows, the pitch of the bottom line chosen there (e.g. E4 – C5 for the melody, E3 – C4 for the accompaniment, E2 – C3 for the bass). The rest of the register is still there to scroll to, the register lock holding if it is on; notes written above or below sit on the white cover with ledger lines (every line from the five lines out to the note, in either line mode), and as soon as the view leaves the five lines a small **↩ LINES** button appears on the cover you are writing on — tap it and the five lines come back. On the phone every stave shows its five lines from the start, and the covers sit behind two **doors** in the page's own colour (dark in the dark theme), each with a lip where it ends and a sliver of the white cover showing beyond the lines; **COVER** beside the stave slides both open at once — one up, one down — and closed again, and they stand open whenever the view is off the five lines. On a wide screen the melody stave has the same doors whenever its five-line focus is on, with COVER in the column beside it.

Stems follow the engraving rule on every stave: below the middle line up, on the head's right; above it down, on the left; on it down. A beamed group follows its note farthest from the middle line, a tie going to the majority, every stem in the group the same way.

## Settings **Note spacing** (Settings → Staves): **Regular**, the bars set in Window shows fill the width; **Small** and **Tight**, every beat no wider than two, or one and a half, of the stave's own staff spaces (and never wider than Regular's), so the spacing complements the view's size and the note size, with as many bars in view as fit at that width, the last running off the edge and the rest a scroll away, on the phone and the deck alike (Stacked staves keep their own bars); the beats stay regular, a beat a beat.

Settings and each stave's ⚙ fold: every section is closed until its **+** opens it (**−** closes it), and every hint sits hidden under the header it explains until its **+** shows it. On the wide screen's rolled-up view only some combinations of bars in view, window sizing, note size and note look display cleanly — one bar: everything but Traditional notes in a Small window; two bars: Micro (but not Micro circles in a Large window), plus Small Traditional notes in a Small window; three and four bars: Micro only — so a choice that would make any other combination is greyed until the other settings permit it, and a saved combination outside the list is set to Micro when the deck opens. With Stacked staves on the wide screen only Micro notes are allowed — four bars in any window and look, eight bars only in a Small window with Traditional notes. The phone is not limited.

## Clef view

Settings → Staves → **Switch to Clef** offers the **Grand Stave, Treble, Alto, Tenor** and **Bass** clefs for every stave at once, and each stave's ⚙ sets that stave's own clef independently of the others; every choice first asks for the song's **key signature and time signature**, both drawn at the stave's head after the clef. In the Clef view the stave is a true letter grid: each row is a letter step and the key signature decides the pitch of every line and space — on the treble staff the space above G is A, on the alto staff the space below the middle line's C is B; a note outside the key sits on its letter's row with a ♯, ♭ or ♮ before it, spelled the way the key leans (F natural in G major is F♮ on the F line, C♯ in C major sits on the C space with a sharp), and the pitch arrows move by half steps, so a note may keep its row with an accidental or step to the next; ledger lines fall every other letter step, middle C one line below the treble staff. A stave slides its five-line grid to the clef's own place — the bottom line E4 for the treble, F3 for the alto, D3 for the tenor, G2 for the bass — with the clef drawn at its head as the engraved glyph, its origin on the line it names — the G clef's curl on the second line, the F clef between its dots on the fourth, the C clef's centre on the third (alto) or fourth (tenor) — sized to the lines, so it grows and shrinks with the spacing but never leans; the key signature's sharps or flats follow on their engraved lines and spaces and the time signature's figures sit centred on the fourth and second lines; notes beyond the five lines get ledger lines out from the nearest staff. The Grand Stave is treble over bass, joined by a brace at the far left, the bar lines running through the gap between them where middle C sits on a ledger line from either staff; the treble staff is the melody and the bass staff carries the **Bass** part as its own voice, drawn there in the bass's red and played together with the melody — a tap on the lower staff enters, selects and edits the Bass part (its own window shows the same notes; with the part switched off the tap opens its settings), a tap on the upper one the melody, and a pitch below the middle of the gap belongs to the lower staff. Leave the Clef view from the same chooser and the staves go back as they were.

## Beside the stave

Under each stave's gear: **⌫** takes back the last note entered on that stave (or the last note of the awake bar), the words untouched, and **UNDO** undoes. **|<>|** and **>|<** set the lines further apart or closer together a step at a time — notes and clef scaling with them, the window's rows growing as they close up — between the legible least and the widest; on the phone they stand where the chord button was, and **CHORD** now sits on the palette's rests row beside ▶ BAR and ▶ SELECT. On the phone the **SELECT, DELETE, REPEAT, COPY** and **MOVE** tools stack beneath them, all one size; **▶ BAR** and **▶ SELECT** sit on the palette's rests row; the play buttons and the tempo live in a pallet at the foot of the page; every note is drawn Micro; the pitch-range arrows ↑ ↓ sit together at the gutter's middle, snug by the pitch marks; and in the rolled-up view the last part in view stays (its − greyed) until another is opened.

## Deleting notes

Tap the beat a note is in, then tap the note itself: it is selected, and **DEL** — a red square with a white outline at the foot of the stave's button column (under the gear on the deck) — appears; it is not there until something is picked. Tap more notes, in any beat or on any stave, and they are picked too; a picked note tapped again is let go, and entering a new note lets them all go. Tap DEL and a small **Delete selected?** asks, Yes or No: every picked note goes, a tied note as one. UNDO brings them back. The trash-can button is gone.

## Chord mode

The two-row button beside each stave turns chord mode on for that stave: every tap at a note's time stacks another note on it **at the length picked in the palette**, whatever is already there (a quarter under two eighths stays a quarter, and the eighths keep their beam); the same pitch again gives a unison (marked with a red edge), and a tap on empty space starts the next chord. A stacked note longer than the rest of its beat is dealt out beat by beat, each piece the largest values that fill it, all tied on — a dotted half dropped on a beat is three tied quarters, a quarter dropped on the "and" is two tied eighths — running into the next bar when it must. Chord-mode notes are drawn in the chord colour (white by default, changeable in Settings) with a thin black edge on head and stem. Rests enter as usual. Tap the button again for single notes.

## Playing

Top row: **|▶ Play All** plays the whole song from the start, **▶… Play Range** asks for a start and end bar and beat and plays that stretch in every part, **■** stops. Beside each stave, **▶** plays that part alone for the current bar, and the **▶ in the blue box** is Play Select: tap it, tap the note heads you want to hear, tap it again — only the chosen notes sound, the rest are greyed, and it stops when they are done or when you press it or ■ again.

## Time signature calculator

☰ → Time signature calculator. Tap note and rest values (whole down to 128th, with dots, double dots and ties) onto a free strip one at a time, hear it tapped out at the song's tempo, then press Calculate: every plausible signature is scored on how the rhythm falls into bars — bar lines landing between entries on struck notes, complete last bars, onsets on the beat, and a preference for regular metres — and the best fits are listed with the rhythm sectioned bar by bar for each.

## Floating tool palette (put away)

The floating tap pad is switched off in the app — nothing opens it and its ⧉ key is not drawn — but its code is kept whole; `FTP_ENABLED` at the top of its section in `index.html` brings it back. When it is on: ⧉ at the end of the tools pops them out into a small draggable palette. The stave keeps its size and stops taking taps; you step through beats (or bars) with ◀ ▶, set the pitch with ▲ ▼, and tap a note length to enter the note at the dashed box, or to change a selected note's length. M / A / B pick the window, ⚙ sets the palette's size and which side the pitch arrows sit, ⤶ pops it back in.

## Where the sound runs out

Each stave wears a red haze over the rows its part's sound cannot really reach — low and high — with the notice “Outside this sound's range — not audible” across it in white small caps, so a note placed there is expected to sound poor or not at all. The reach comes from the instrument the built-in sound imitates (a voice from F2 to C6, a bass guitar from E1 to G4, a piano the whole keyboard, and so on); a sample bank reaches a fifth beyond its recorded notes either way.

## Running without the browser bars

- **Android / desktop:** ☰ → *Full screen* hides the address bar (tap again, or press Esc, to leave).
- **Any phone, best option:** add the page to your Home Screen (Chrome: ⋮ → *Add to Home screen*; iPhone Safari: Share → *Add to Home Screen*). The icon then opens the app on its own, with no address bar in either orientation. `manifest.webmanifest` and `icons/` provide the name and icon for that.

## Real instrument sounds

Menu → *Sounds & samples* imports instrument recordings from your device into the browser's IndexedDB; nothing is downloaded by the app. Each part then picks its sound in Band (built-in synth or a sample bank).

- **tonejs-instruments** — the `samples` folder from <https://github.com/nbrosowsky/tonejs-instruments> (guitar-acoustic, guitar-electric, bass-electric, piano, trumpet, clarinet, tuba, violin, cello, french-horn …). Pick the whole folder and every instrument becomes a bank; matching parts are assigned automatically.
- **Salamander Grand Piano** (CC BY 3.0, Alexander Holm) — the full multi-layer set or the trimmed one at <https://tonejs.github.io/audio/salamander/>. One velocity layer per note is kept (nearest v8).

File names must carry the pitch: `C4.mp3`, `Cs4.mp3`, `F#3.wav`, `A0v8.mp3`. Prefer mp3; iPhones don't decode ogg.

## Data pipeline

After editing the workbook:

```bash
python compile_data.py genre_chord_bank_v9.xlsx song_data.js --embed index.html
```

Requires `openpyxl`. The compiler validates genre / chord cross-references and prints warnings; a clean run means the workbook is internally consistent.
